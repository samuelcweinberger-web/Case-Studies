Pricing & Packaging a Direct-to-Consumer Streaming Subscription

Features alone didn’t explain what fans would pay for—a measure of what motivates fans to subscribe explained why segments chose differently.

1.1M Sign-ups at Launch
~2.7M Subscribers by 2024

Situation

  • The league was preparing its first direct-to-consumer mobile subscription, in a field that already included several of its existing subscription products.
  • Before launch, product and media strategy needed evidence — not instinct — for how to package and price it.

Task

  • Evaluate five different ways the subscription could be packaged.
  • Explain why different fan segments preferred different packages, using a measure of what actually motivates fans to subscribe.

Action

  • Ran Design Studios with 24 fans, using MoSCoW sorting (Must / Should / Could / Won’t Have) and package ranking both before and after fans saw the prices.
  • Fielded a national survey of 2,208 fans on the features they wanted and the competitor services they already used.
  • Analyzed which features had the broadest appeal (a TURF-style reach analysis) and what most motivated subscription (MaxDiff), all in SPSS.

Result

  • The research shaped the league’s first-ever mobile subscription. The packaging and pricing recommendations became the basis for the initial offering.
  • It launched to about 1.1 million sign-ups in 2022 and grew to roughly 2.7 million subscribers heading into 2024.
  • It was a cross-team effort. The packaging blueprint was built in partnership with the media and product strategy teams.
